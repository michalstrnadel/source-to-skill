# 10: Future of education: Eureka, ramps to knowledge, how to teach and learn (1:56:20–2:25:14)

Chapter "Future of education", about Karpathy's education project Eureka.

## Why education and not another AI lab

- He feels "some amount of determinism" in what the AI labs are doing. He could help, but not
  uniquely. **His big fear is AI happening "on the side of humanity"** with humans
  disempowered. He cares about what happens to humans, not just the Dyson spheres, and fears a
  *WALL-E* or *Idiocracy* future. Education is where he can add unique value. [1:56:37](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=6997) [1:57:08](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7028)
- **Eureka is "trying to build the Starfleet Academy"**: an elite, up-to-date institution
  for frontier technical knowledge. [1:57:39](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7059)

## The bar: a great human tutor

- Today's obvious move (an LLM you ask questions) helps but "still feels... a bit slop".
  The capability for a real tutor isn't there yet. [1:58:10](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7090) [1:58:40](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7120)
- **Korean-learning story:** he went from self-study online, to a ~10-person class in Korea, to a one-on-one
  tutor. From a short conversation she understood what he knew, probed his world model,
  and always served material at the right difficulty, never too hard or too trivial. **"No LLM will do that for you
  100% right now, not even close."** With her, "I was the only constraint to learning." - **So it's not the right time to build the full AI tutor**, though people will build

## What Eureka is building now

- First, a state-of-the-art course on AI, the field he knows. **nanochat is the capstone
  project of LLM101n**, the class he's building. He still needs the intermediate material and a small
  team of TAs. [2:01:32](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7292) [2:02:03](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7323)
- **Education is "the very difficult technical process of building ramps to
  knowledge."** The target metric is **"Eurekas per second"** (understanding per second):
  learners should never be stuck and never bored. [2:02:33](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7353) [2:03:04](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=7384)
- **AI's role today is collaborator, not author.** Asking ChatGPT to "teach me AI" gives slop, and "AI is
  never going to write nanochat right now". Compared with building CS231n at Stanford (which he
  believes was its first deep learning class), LLM101n goes much faster with LLMs doing the boring
  parts, but he still does the creative content. Keep calibrating to what exists. ## Pre-AGI vs. post-AGI education

- **"Pre-AGI education is useful, post-AGI education is fun"**, like the gym. We don't need
  physical strength to move heavy things, yet people lift because it's fun, healthy and
  attractive. Learning is hard today and people bounce off material. A perfect tutor is a technical,
  tractable problem, and it would make learning anything trivial and desirable. ## How to teach (advice for educators)

- **Physics "boots up the brain".** Everyone should learn it early: building models and abstractions,
  first-, second- and third-order terms, abstracting a noisy system to its fundamental
  frequencies. The "spherical cow" is "brilliant thinking". He recommends the book *Scale*
  (a physicist on biology), which charts animal scaling laws such as heat dissipation growing with surface area
  (squared) while heat generation grows with volume (cubed). [2:14:41](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8081) [2:15:14](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8114) [2:15:45](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8145) [2:16:16](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8176)
- **Find the first-order term and serve it on a platter.** **micrograd** is 100 lines of
  Python that do forward and backward passes over a graph of simple ops (plus, times): the
  recursive chain rule. "Everything else is just efficiency" (tensors, strides, kernels,
  memory movement). [2:16:46](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8206) [2:17:17](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8237) [2:17:47](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8267)
- **Untangle knowledge into a ramp where everything depends only on what came before.**
  Dwarkesh: his transformer tutorial starts from a bigram lookup table. Karpathy: **present the pain before the
  solution**, so every piece is motivated. - **Let the student guess first.** Showing the solution before they've tried is "a dick move". Trying

## How to learn (advice for students)

- No unique tricks: "it's kind of a painful process". **Alternate depth-wise, on-demand learning**
  (a project you'll get a reward from) with **breadth-wise learning** (school's "trust me, you'll
  need this later"). He loves the on-demand kind. [2:23:13](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8593) [2:23:45](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8625)
- **Explain things to others.** "If I don't really understand something I can't explain it."
  Explaining exposes gaps and forces you to reconcile them. [2:24:15](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8655) [2:24:47](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=8687)
