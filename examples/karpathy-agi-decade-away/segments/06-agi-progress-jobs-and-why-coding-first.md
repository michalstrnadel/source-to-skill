## Radiologists vs. call centers

- **Geoff Hinton's prediction that radiologists would disappear "turned out to be very
  wrong".** Radiologists are "alive and well and growing", even though computer vision is good at
  recognition, because the job is messy (patients, many surfaces). By that definition AI hasn't
  made a huge dent yet. [1:08:47](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4127) [1:09:17](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4157)
- **Call-center work is the candidate for early automation.** It is a sequence of similar,
  short tasks (~10 minutes per call, editing database entries), with little context, closed and
  purely digital. Look for a short task horizon and little context. [1:09:17](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4157) [1:09:49](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4189) [1:10:00](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4200)
- **Autonomy slider, not replacement:** expect AIs doing **80% of the volume, delegating
  20% to humans, with humans supervising teams of five AIs**. Opportunity: new interfaces
  or companies that let you manage imperfect AIs. [1:10:30](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4230)
- Dwarkesh's counter-model: Waymo safety drivers and Tesla Robotaxi still carry a person. If
  99% of a job is automated, the last 1% bottlenecks everything and wages could rise. Karpathy
  doesn't see that with radiology, which he thinks is "not a good example". He doesn't know why Hinton
  picked it. He'd watch call-center trends instead, and expects some companies to swap in AI and then
  pull back and rehire within a year or two. [1:11:00](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4260) [1:11:31](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4291) [1:12:02](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4322) [1:12:32](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4352)

## Why coding first

- Dwarkesh: AGI isn't taking little tasks from consultants and accountants. API revenue
  is "dominated by coding". [1:13:03](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4383) [1:13:34](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4414) [1:14:05](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4445)
- Karpathy: **coding is "the perfect first thing"** for LLMs and agents. Code is text,
  terminals are text, and LLMs "love text". Lots of infrastructure is already built:
  IDEs, and diffs that show an agent's changes. [1:14:05](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4445) [1:14:35](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4475)
- **Counter-example: slides.** They are spatial graphics, and nothing shows a diff of a slide
  deck. "Someone has to build it." [1:15:07](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4507) [1:15:37](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4537)
- Dwarkesh pushes back that pure language tasks are hard too. He has struggled with transcript
  rewriting and clips, and their mutual friend Andy Matuschak tried in-context
  examples, supervised fine-tuning and retrieval but couldn't get models to write spaced-repetition
  cards to his satisfaction. [1:15:37](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4537) [1:16:08](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4568) [1:16:39](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4599)
- Karpathy: code is structured, while prose has more entropy. Text helps, but "it doesn't mean that all
  text is trivial". He admits he has no very good answer. [1:17:10](https://api.substack.com/feed/podcast/176425744/3127b612a1604474d2b28ab4e9b658f4.mp3#t=4630)
